Notation

A key to the sigla used in the manuscript: the glyphs that notate a Bitcoin script's opcodes, and the marks drawn in each transaction's margins. Pushed data — keys, hashes, signatures — is rendered as prose between the glyphs.

Signatures & keys

∇check a signature
▼check a signature, else fail
◇check m-of-n signatures
◆check m-of-n, else fail

A triangle is one signer, a diamond is many; a filled shape must hold or the script fails.

Hashes

⌖address hash · HASH160
⌘identity hash · HASH256
ΣSHA-256
σSHA-1
ρRIPEMD-160

Lower-case Greek is a 160-bit digest, upper-case a 256-bit one. ⌖ points to where value is sent; ⌘ marks what names a transaction.

Timelocks

τabsolute — not before a fixed time or block
Δrelative — a delay after this coin confirmed

τ is a fixed point in time; Δ is an interval measured from the moment the coin was received.

Numbers

⓪push zero
①–⑯push a small number (1–16)
⊖push minus one

Common script patterns

A payment's kind is legible in its sequence of marks — learn these and you can read a script's purpose at sight. ‹…› is pushed data (rendered as prose).

P2PK‹key› ∇ P2PKH⧉ ⌖ ‹…› ≡ ∇ P2SH⌖ ‹…› = P2WPKH⓪ ‹20-byte …› P2WSH⓪ ‹32-byte …› P2TR① ‹key› Multisig② ‹key›‹key›‹key› ③ ◇ Data¶ ‹data›

P2WPKH and P2WSH share the mark ⓪ — a witness-v0 program — and differ only in the hashed length: a 20-byte HASH160 of a key, or a 32-byte SHA-256 of a script. P2TR opens with ① instead. The scripts hidden inside P2SH / P2WSH — an m-of-n ◇, or an HTLC ⟨ Σ ‹…› ≡ … │ … τ ⌄ … ⟩ — are revealed in the same notation when spent.

Margin — the transaction's locktime & other marks

□no locktime
■nnot before block n
⊥nnot before time n
∅a coinbase — no prior output
§nverse — the transaction's position

■ and ⊥ mean block-height and time in both places; a square reads as the whole transaction, a circle as one input.

About

Each transaction's base bytes (version, inputs, outputs, locktime — exactly what a txid hashes) become a paragraph, each input's witness a numbered footnote. Small structural numbers (version, indices, sequence, amounts, locktime) appear as literal digits, and scripts are written in the opcode notation of the Notation key above.

Only genuine payload becomes prose. Serialization framing — input/output and witness-item counts, and push/item length prefixes — is dropped, since it is reconstructable from structure. And a canonical ECDSA signature is stripped from its DER envelope down to just its r, s and sighash flag; a non-canonical, pre-BIP66 signature fails the re-encode check and is kept verbatim, so its bytes still reconstruct exactly. What remains in prose is the genuinely opaque material: prevout references, keys, hashes, and signature scalars.
